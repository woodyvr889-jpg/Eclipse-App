About This Eclipse

On 12 August 2026, a total solar eclipse will cross parts of the UK. Northern Scotland will experience totality, while the rest of England and Wales will see a deep partial eclipse. It's the most significant eclipse visible from the UK in decades.

🛡 Safety Guide

🚫

Never Look Directly at the Sun

Looking directly at the sun — even during a partial eclipse — can cause permanent eye damage or blindness within seconds. Only the brief moment of totality (in the path of totality) is safe for naked-eye viewing.

✅

Use Certified Eclipse Glasses

Only use glasses that meet the ISO 12312-2 international standard. Look for the ISO certification printed on the frame. These filter out 99.997% of sunlight.

❌

Do NOT Use Normal Sunglasses

Standard sunglasses, even very dark ones, are completely inadequate for solar viewing. They do not filter harmful infrared and UV radiation.

📱

Phone Cameras Need Protection Too

Pointing your phone camera at the sun without a solar filter can damage the camera sensor. Use a solar filter film over the lens when photographing the partial phases.

📸

How to Safely Photograph the Eclipse

  • Attach a solar filter (ND5 or solar film) to your camera/phone lens
  • Use a tripod for stability
  • Set a low ISO (100–200) and fast shutter speed
  • During totality only: remove filter briefly for corona shots
  • Replace filter immediately when totality ends
🔭

Safe Viewing Alternatives

  • Pinhole projector — project the sun's image through a small hole onto paper
  • Colander projection — hold a kitchen colander above a white surface
  • Solar telescope — with proper solar filter attached
  • Watch the live stream — completely safe from your screen
